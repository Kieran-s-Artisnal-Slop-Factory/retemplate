Baths shut in March. Council knew in May.

By Marta Okonkwo, chief reporter · Additional reporting by Rhys Calloway

Ashcombe Baths will close on 2 March for at least eighteen months, the council confirmed last night, after the Bugle obtained a structural survey describing the roof trusses as "beyond economic repair". The survey is dated 11 May. Councillors were briefed on 17 June. The swimming club, which trains there five mornings a week, found out on Monday when a laminated notice went up by the lockers giving a closing date and a phone number that rings out.

The report, by engineers Harte and Pell, gives the building two winters at most and recommends that the pool be drained before the second. Repair is costed at £4.1m. A new pool at Mill Field is costed at £11m and would open, on the engineers' own timetable, in 2029. The council has budgeted for neither.

Asked why the closure had not been announced in June, the council's leader, Gwen Tallis, said the "options had to be understood first". Asked what the two hundred children in the swimming club would do, she said the club "had been made aware". The club told the Bugle it had not. Its secretary, Tom Achebe, learned of the closure from a parent who had read the notice.

Two winters is not a forecast. It is a deadline the council chose not to mention.

Tom Achebe, Ashcombe Swimming Club

What closes, and when

  • 2 March: the pool closes to the public. The gym on the first floor stays open until the roof work starts.
  • April to October 2027: the trusses are replaced, or the building is stripped for demolition. The council will decide which on 25 September.
  • 2028, or 2029: a pool reopens, "subject to funding", which is the phrase the survey uses for the £4.1m and the £11m alike.

The club

Ashcombe Swimming Club is the only club in the district with its own lanes. Without a pool its 210 members train at Hadley, fourteen miles away, in a pool that has already told the club it can offer two mornings, not five. The school swimming programme, which uses the baths on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons, has no alternative at all.

The council says it will "work with the club to find a solution" and expects to say more "in the coming weeks". Term started on Tuesday.

In numbers

116 days between the survey and the notice · 210 club members · 14 miles to the next pool · £4.1m to repair · £11m to rebuild · 0 pounds budgeted

What happens next

The council's cabinet meets on 25 September. The Bugle has asked for the survey to be published in full, and will publish it ourselves if it is not. Late last night the council said it would release the document at 6pm today.

Leader

Publish the report. Then fix the roof.

A roof that has two winters left is not a secret. It is a maintenance schedule, and the people who use the building underneath it are the first who should have been told. The council had a summer to plan for the club, the schools and the Tuesday lane swimmers. It spent that summer understanding its options, and the options have not changed since May.

Publish the survey, all of it. Then tell the town which of the two prices it intends to pay and how. Ashcombe has paid for a pool since 1934 and will pay for one again; what it will not forgive is finding out from a laminated sign by the lockers.
