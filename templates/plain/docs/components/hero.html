Hero

The opening of a page: a heading at the largest size, a paragraph, one or two buttons, and beside them whatever the template leads with. Every template has its own hero form; this page shows Plain's, then two variants that move.

Preview

The hero as the overview uses it. The heading takes --font-size-hero; the paragraph is muted and capped at a readable measure.

<section class="hero">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">The starting point</p>
    <h1>Plain is the template the other templates are made from.</h1>
    <p>Two stylesheets, one small script, no build step. Every component the retemplate contract asks for, in a quiet style you can take as it is or re-skin into something of your own.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg" href="index.html">Browse components</a>
    </div>
  </div>

</section>

Variants

Text only

The same hero with nothing beside the words. Add hero-text: the grid becomes one column and the stack is capped at a readable measure. Every template carries this variant with the same class.

<section class="hero hero-text">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">Words only</p>
    <h1>A hero with nothing beside it.</h1>
    <p>One column, the text measure capped so the lines stay readable, and everything else the hero has: the kicker, the heading at the hero size, the paragraph, the buttons.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg" href="index.html">Browse components</a>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

Image led

A hero that gives the picture most of the room: the words take one column and the image three, a 25/75 split at desktop widths that stacks on a phone. Add hero-image-half as well for an even 50/50. Every template carries this variant with the same classes.

<section class="hero hero-image">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">Picture led</p>
    <h1>The picture takes three quarters.</h1>
    <p>Words in the first column, the picture in the rest.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
    </div>
  </div>

</section>

The reveal

Text first, picture second. Each line of the stack rises into place a beat after the one before, a rule draws itself under the heading, and the picture fades in last. It runs once, on load. Add plain-reveal to the hero. Under prefers-reduced-motion the global rule cuts every animation to nothing, so the hero is simply there.

<section class="hero plain-reveal">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">The starting point</p>
    <h1>Every line arrives in its turn.</h1>
    <p>The kicker, the heading, this paragraph and the buttons rise one after another; the rule under the heading draws itself; the picture comes last.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg" href="index.html">Browse components</a>
    </div>
  </div>

</section>

The roll

The last word of the heading is a short list that rolls upward: each word slides into place, holds, and gives way to the next, on a loop. The list is markup, so the words are readable and copyable, and the roll is one keyframe on the list. Add plain-roll to the hero; the global prefers-reduced-motion rule stops it.

<section class="hero plain-roll">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">The starting point</p>
    <h1>Plain is <span class="plain-roll-words"><span>quiet.</span><span>yours.</span><span>enough.</span><span>quiet.</span></span></h1>
    <p>Three words take turns in the heading. The last entry repeats the first so the loop closes without a jump.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg" href="index.html">Browse components</a>
    </div>
  </div>

</section>

Classes

The rules live in css/global.css: the hero under /* == utilities == */ with the other layout helpers, the variant under /* == hero variants == */.

ClassPurpose
.heroThe opening section: a grid of a text stack and, in most templates, a picture or a piece of flare
.hero .stackThe kicker, heading, paragraph and button row
.hero .rowThe buttons
.hero.plain-revealThe staggered rise; the heading draws its rule, the picture fades in last
.hero.plain-rollThe heading with a rolling word
.plain-roll-wordsThe clipped column; its spans are the words, last repeating first
.hero.hero-textWords only: one column, capped measure (every template)
.hero.hero-imagePicture led: words one column, picture three; with .hero-image-half an even split (every template)

Notes

  • One <h1> per page: the hero's heading is it. The kicker above it is a <p>, not a heading.
  • Above 48rem the hero is two columns, text then picture; below, it stacks. The variant keeps whatever layout its markup implies.
  • The variant is CSS only. Its animation is declared with both fill, so the page is correct before it starts and after it ends, and the global prefers-reduced-motion rule stops it.
  • Another template ignores plain-reveal and shows its own hero; any flare markup inside sits in the template's flare wrapper so a swap can drop it.
