Hart's-tongue fern

The one fern that does not look like a fern.

An undivided, strap-shaped frond with a gloss on it, evergreen through the worst of the winter. It grows in the cracks of old walls and the mouths of wells, and it will grow for you in any shade that never quite dries out.

Growing it

Pl. XII

Cool ground, and leave it be

Plant it where the soil is coolest: the foot of a north wall, the shady side of a step, beneath a hedge. It does not want rich soil so much as soil that never bakes; a handful of grit and a mulch of leaf mould in autumn is all the feeding it needs. Cut the old fronds away in late winter before the new ones unroll.

In a dry spell it will droop and look finished. Water it and it stands up again by morning. The one thing it will not forgive is a summer of full sun.

Its neighbours

From the collecting book

The spore lines on the back of the frond run in pairs, like the legs of a centipede, which is what scolopendrium means. They ripen in late summer. Sow them onto a damp brick under a jar and you will have a green film by autumn and, if you are patient and lucky, a fern in two years.

If a wall is old enough to have a fern in it, it is old enough to be left alone.

14 May

Six new fronds on the barn plants since the rain. Spores ripe on last year's growth; sowed a tray onto damp brick and covered it.
