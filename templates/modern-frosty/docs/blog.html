Blog anatomy

The post shell

A blog post is the fixture article inside a shell: a header with the author card, the body in .prose, and a footer. The blog post page is the full thing; this is the skeleton.

The skeleton

The <article> inside .post-body is where rendered markdown goes; in the showcase it is the shared fixture, verbatim. The author card is its own component (docs); the kicker, meta and tags are utilities.

<article class="post">
  <header class="post-header">
    <p class="kicker">Section · Topic</p>
    <aside class="author-card">
      <img class="avatar" src="../assets/avatar.svg" alt="" width="96" height="96">
      <div>
        <strong class="author-name">Author name</strong>
        <p class="author-bio">One line about the author.</p>
        <p class="meta"><time datetime="2026-09-04">4 September 2026</time> · <span>3 min read</span></p>
        <p class="tags"><a class="tag" href="#">tag</a><a class="tag" href="#">another</a></p>
      </div>
    </aside>
  </header>
  <div class="prose post-body">
    <article>
      <h1>The post title</h1>
      <p>The body of the post: rendered markdown, wrapped in <code>.prose</code>.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
  <footer class="post-footer">
    <hr class="page-break">
    <p class="muted">Related posts, previous and next, or nothing at all.</p>
  </footer>
</article>

What the shell styles

ClassPurpose
.postThe article wrapper; no styling of its own
.post-headerSpacing above the body; an h1 here gets the hero size
.post-bodyPairs with .prose; the markdown lives inside
.post-footerSpacing below the body
.kicker, .meta, .tags, .tagSmall text above and below the title

Where the title goes

Rendered markdown usually begins with its own <h1>, as the fixture does, so the shell leaves the title to the body. If your renderer strips the title out, put an <h1> in .post-header instead; it is styled for it.
