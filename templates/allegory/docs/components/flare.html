Flare

Flare is everything a template adds beyond the contract. Allegory adds one idea, the two faces, and a few pieces that use it: a pair of classes that pin an element to one face, a split section that shows both, a band of display type, a verse, and a button in italic display type. The radiance behind the page by day and the flame by night are not flare; they are tokens on <body> and need no markup.

The two faces

allegory-halo pins an element to the day face and allegory-horn to the night face. Each sets color-scheme on the element, so every light-dark() pair inside resolves to that half whatever the visitor chose, native controls included, and re-points the ornament mask, which is the one thing a colour function cannot swap. A pinned block is padded and edged like a panel; a pinned card stays a card. Flip the toggle: these two do not move.

<div class="grid-2">
  <section class="allegory-halo">
    <h3 class="allegory-band">By day</h3>
    <p>Ivory, gold, wings. Everything inside is the day face: text, links, <a href="#">this link</a>, and the field below.</p>
    <div class="field"><label for="f-day">A field, pinned light</label><input id="f-day" type="text" placeholder="Native controls follow the pin"></div>
  </section>
  <section class="allegory-horn">
    <h3 class="allegory-band">By night</h3>
    <p>Black, red, horns. Everything inside is the night face: text, links, <a href="#">this link</a>, and the field below.</p>
    <div class="field"><label for="f-night">A field, pinned dark</label><input id="f-night" type="text" placeholder="Native controls follow the pin"></div>
  </section>
</div>

The diptych

A split section: one grid, two panels, each pinned to a face, each opening with its ornament drawn by ::before. The overview's hero is one of these. On a narrow screen the panels stack, day above night. Any children work; the pattern is a band, a paragraph and a vow.

<div class="allegory-diptych">
  <section class="allegory-halo">
    <h2 class="allegory-band">The ascent</h2>
    <p>Mara Quill climbs the tower to ring the hour.</p>
    <a class="btn allegory-vow" href="../../example/ascent.html">Read the ascent</a>
  </section>
  <section class="allegory-horn">
    <h2 class="allegory-band">The descent</h2>
    <p>Tobin Marrow goes down the well to find the hum.</p>
    <a class="btn allegory-vow" href="../../example/descent.html">Read the descent</a>
  </section>
</div>

The band

The literal white-on-gold and black-on-red of the brief, kept for display type. Put allegory-band on a heading and it becomes a strip of the band colour at that heading's size; on any other element the type is forced to at least 24px bold. Body text never goes on a band: the contrast is not there for it.

<div class="stack">
  <h2 class="allegory-band">A heading on the band</h2>
  <p class="allegory-band">A paragraph, made display type by the class. <em>Italic is lighter.</em></p>
</div>

The verse

A quotation set centred in the display italic, with the ornament above it in the band colour. Use it for an epigraph or a pull quote; the ordinary <blockquote> inside .prose is for quotations in running text. The <cite> is optional.

<blockquote class="allegory-verse">
  <p>Every bell that lifts a soul is rung by someone standing in the dark below it.</p>
  <cite>The Bell at Corren, chapter one</cite>
</blockquote>

The vow

A button in italic display type with a band-coloured outline: a promise rather than a control. On hover and focus it fills with the accent, which clears AA with its text, rather than the band, which would not. It takes btn-lg.

<div class="row">
  <button class="btn allegory-vow" type="button">I will finish the chapter</button>
  <a class="btn allegory-vow btn-lg" href="#">Read the ascent</a>
</div>

Classes

The rules live in css/global.css under /* == flare == */.

ClassPurpose
.allegory-flareThe wrapper for flare that is only decoration; strip it when swapping templates
.allegory-haloPin to the day face: color-scheme: light, wings
.allegory-hornPin to the night face: color-scheme: dark, horns
.card.allegory-halo, .card.allegory-hornA pinned card; signs off with its ornament at the foot
.allegory-diptychTwo-column grid of pinned panels; one column below 48rem; ornament by ::before
.allegory-bandDisplay type on the band colour; headings keep their size, anything else is at least 24px bold
.allegory-verseCentred display-italic quotation with the ornament above; cite inside is the attribution
.btn.allegory-vowOutlined button in italic display type; fills with the accent on hover

Notes

  • Every colour here is a token. The band is --allegory-band and --allegory-on-band; the two faces written out flat are --allegory-day-* and --allegory-night-*; the ornaments are --allegory-wings and --allegory-horns, chosen by --allegory-ornament. All in theme.css, listed on the theme page. Nothing in global.css names a colour.
  • Nothing here animates. The only motion in the template is the feature card's turn and a short fade when the scheme changes, both transitions the global prefers-reduced-motion rule shortens to nothing.
  • A pin is color-scheme, so inside a pinned element the browser draws form controls, scrollbars and the dialog backdrop in that scheme too.
  • Inside the .themed swatch panels on the theme page the ornament does not swap, because those panels pin color-scheme without setting data-scheme. Accepted; pin with allegory-halo or allegory-horn instead and it does.
  • The diptych and the band are content, not decoration, so they do not need aria-hidden. The ornaments drawn by ::before and ::after are invisible to assistive technology already.
