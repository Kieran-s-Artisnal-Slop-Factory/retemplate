Chapter one, upward

The ascent

Mara Quill has forty minutes, two hundred and twelve steps, a lamp with an hour of oil in it, and a knee that has opinions about all three.

The stair does not care who climbs it. That is the kindest thing about a stair.

Mara Quill, on the ninetieth step

The tower

Two hundred and twelve steps, and she knows every one

The first sixty are stone and wide. The next hundred are oak and narrow, and turn. The last fifty-two are a ladder that the town council has been meaning to replace since 1974. Mara counts them going up because it is better than thinking about the bell rope.

Now read the descent

The climb

The stone stair

Wide enough for two, worn into a shallow trough by four centuries of bellkeepers. The lamp throws her shadow ahead of her, and it reaches the landing first.

The oak stair

Here the tower narrows and the steps begin to turn. At the hundred and fortieth she hears it for the first time: a hum, below her, steady as a held note.

The ladder

Fifty-two rungs into the bell chamber. The hum is louder up here, which makes no sense, unless the whole tower is the string and the bell is the thing that plucks it.

People on the stair

Idris Vane, the verger

Waits at the foot of the stone stair with a second lamp she will not need and a flask she will. He has offered to climb in her place every year for nineteen years, and every year she has said the same thing.

The council's ladder inspector

Never met, only read. His notice is nailed at the top of the oak stair: Ladder condition: adequate. Re-inspect: 1975. Mara pats it as she passes, the way you pat a horse.

The bell, Patience

Four tonnes of bronze that has not been late in four hundred years. Tonight it is warm to the touch, and there is no reason for that at all.

Write to Mara

Readers send her things: knee advice, mostly. She reads all of it on the landing at step sixty, where there is a bench.

Optional. She answers about one in three.
