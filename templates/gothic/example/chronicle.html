Chronicle of the chapter · Entry 217

The fall of the north-west tower, Candlemas 1471

Transcribed from the chapter's own hand by Margery Thorne, keeper of the archive. Spelling modernised; nothing else touched. The notes in the margin are hers.

On the eve of Candlemas, a little after the bell for Compline, the north-west tower of this church fell. It took with it two bays of the aisle roof and the greater part of the west porch. The canons were in the choir; the sacrist, Brother Oswy, was in the porch and lived, having gone back for his lantern. No one was killed. The bells lie in the yard.

The chapter met the next morning in the vestry, the chapter house being full of dust, and resolved three things. That the fabric roll be read aloud before any new work is agreed. That the mason Roger Ashe be sent for from the quarry at Kelmscar. That this book be begun, so that what is decided is written down the same week and not remembered differently in a year.

Paid to twelve men for the clearing of the yard, eight days, 16s. Paid for candles for the same, the days being short, 14d.

The rebuilding took eleven years. Ashe's tower is a course taller than the old one and its windows are wider, which is why the two western towers do not match and never will. The bells went back up in 1482; the tenor cracked in the fall and was recast at Hollowmere by the founder William Caster, whose mark is on it still.

The entry ends with the names of the canons present, in the order they sat. Seven of the nine signed. The other two, the chronicle says, were at Hollowmere Bridge on the chapter's business and signed on their return.

SourceChapter act book, volume 2, folios 3 to 5.
