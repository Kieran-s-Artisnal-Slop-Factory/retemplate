Component

Flare

Flare is everything a template adds beyond the contract. Gothic adds four things from a scriptorium: an illuminated initial, a manuscript page with notes in the margin, a rose window, and an arcade rule. Two more are variants of contract components and live on their own pages: the arched card and the tracery rule. The pointed arch itself is a token, --gothic-arch, and needs no markup.

Illuminated initial

Put gothic-initial on a paragraph and its first letter is set large in blackletter, crimson by day and gold by night, on a violet ground inside a double rule. It is a ::first-letter rule, so the paragraph's markup does not change. One per page is enough; the overview and the example put it on the first paragraph of the hero.

<p class="gothic-initial">In the year of the great frost the chapter met by candlelight, the glass in the north windows having cracked, and resolved that the fabric roll be read aloud before any new work was begun.</p>

Manuscript with marginalia

A two-column page: the text on the left, notes on the right in a small italic with a crimson rule. Above 48rem the notes are a 13rem column; below it they follow the text. A <strong> at the start of a note is its heading, in small caps. The wrapper is gothic-flare, so a template swap can remove the whole thing.

<div class="gothic-flare">
  <div class="gothic-manuscript">
    <div class="prose">
      <p class="gothic-initial">The north-west tower fell on the eve of Candlemas, 1471, taking two bays of the aisle roof with it. No one was killed. The chapter's account, written the same week, is the only record.</p>
      <p>The rebuilding took eleven years and is the reason the two western towers do not match.</p>
    </div>
    <aside class="gothic-marginalia">
      <p><strong>Source</strong>Fabric roll 1470/71, membrane 4.</p>
      <p><strong>Shelf mark</strong>D/C/F/12.</p>
      <p><strong>See also</strong>Entry 218, the mason's contract.</p>
    </aside>
  </div>
</div>

Rose window

An inline SVG drawing sized by gothic-rose: eight petals in the ornament colour and violet, with lead lines. It is decoration, so mark it aria-hidden. The example's visiting page puts one above the hours table.

Arcade rule

A row of small pointed arches the full width of the column, for the places where a page changes subject. It is an <hr> with a class, so a template swap leaves an ordinary rule behind. The page break is the other ornament: a quatrefoil, centred, for a break inside one subject.

<p>The hours.</p>
<hr class="gothic-tracery">
<p>The rules of the search room.</p>

Classes

The rules live in css/global.css under /* == flare == */, except the two variants, which sit with their components.

ClassPurpose
.gothic-flareThe wrapper for flare that is markup; strip it when swapping templates
.gothic-initialIlluminated first letter on a paragraph (::first-letter)
.gothic-manuscriptTwo-column grid: text and margin
.gothic-marginaliaThe margin column: small italic, crimson rule, small-caps headings
.gothic-roseSizes and colours an inline rose-window SVG
hr.gothic-traceryArcade rule (global.css § page-break)
.card.gothic-archLancet picture in a card (global.css § cards)

Notes

  • Every colour here is a token: --gothic-ornament, --gothic-violet, --gothic-violet-soft, --gothic-tracery, --gothic-rule and --gothic-arch in theme.css. Nothing in global.css names a colour.
  • Nothing here moves. The only motion in the template is the stained-glass wash on the feature card and the arch marker on an accordion, both transitions the global prefers-reduced-motion rule shortens to nothing.
  • The initial and the manuscript are content and should not be hidden from assistive technology. The rose window is decoration; mark it aria-hidden="true" as the example does.
  • Blackletter has no bold and no italic. The initial uses the face at its one weight; do not put gothic-initial on text that is already bold.
