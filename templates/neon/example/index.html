What Relay does

Checks from nine regions

HTTP, TCP, DNS and heartbeat monitors every thirty seconds. A single region going quiet is noted; three agreeing is an incident.

Incidents that write themselves

The timeline starts the moment a check fails and fills in as regions recover. You add the human sentence; Relay keeps the timestamps.

Subscribers, not refreshers

Email, RSS, webhook and Slack. Subscribers hear about a fix at the same second you do, and never see a page that says "investigating" for six hours.

Honesty by design

The light only goes green when we can prove it

A monitor shows operational when every region has succeeded in the last two minutes. Anything less is degraded, in amber, with the failing regions listed. There is no setting to change that.

Questions people ask before they sign up

Can I put the status page on my own domain?

Yes, on every plan including the free one. Point a CNAME at us and Relay issues the certificate.

What happens when Relay itself goes down?

The status pages are served from a static edge that does not depend on the checking fleet, and our own status page is run by a competitor. We think that is the only honest arrangement.

Do you store request bodies?

No. We keep status codes, timings and the first 512 bytes of a failed response, for fourteen days, in the region you choose.
