Component

Flare

Flare is everything a template adds beyond the contract. Glassy adds the colour behind the glass (the orbs), the strip the hero stands over (the waves), the sheet hero itself, and a way of composing a page by depth: three tiers any sheet can take, a float that pulls a block up over the one before it, a pane for grouping small parts, and a stack for a few panels in order. The variants of contract components (depth on cards, the ghost button, the frost banner, the price table, the overlapped fifty-fifty, the floating navbar) are documented on those components' pages and listed at the bottom of this one.

Orbs

Two large blurred discs, fixed behind the page, drifting slowly. Put the block right after <body>; it is aria-hidden and never intercepts a click. Without them the glass has nothing to show through it, so every page in the showcase carries them. (The preview below is inert because it sits inside the page rather than behind it.)

<div class="glassy-flare glassy-orbs" aria-hidden="true">
  <div class="glassy-orb"></div>
  <div class="glassy-orb"></div>
</div>

The sheet hero

Add glassy-sheet to .hero and it becomes one deep pane of glass, text only, at most 44rem wide and centred. Put a wave strip directly after it and the strip is pulled up under the sheet's lower edge, so the sheet stands over the water. The overview and the studio home both open this way; the sheet is the template's signature.

<section class="hero glassy-sheet">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">A design studio of four, Kendal</p>
    <h1>Websites you can read, from people you can ring.</h1>
    <p>Fixed prices, plain HTML, and the same two people from the first call to the last deploy.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="#">Start a project</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg glassy-ghost" href="#">Services and prices</a>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

Waves

One path, four <use> copies, four different durations. Because the path has an id, use the block once per page, and use it after a sheet hero; on its own it is a strip of moving water with nothing standing on it.

<div class="glassy-flare glassy-waves" aria-hidden="true">
  <svg viewBox="0 24 150 28" preserveAspectRatio="none">
    <defs><path id="glassy-wave" d="M-160 44c30 0 58-18 88-18s58 18 88 18 58-18 88-18 58 18 88 18v44h-352z"/></defs>
    <g class="glassy-wave-set">
      <use href="#glassy-wave" x="48" y="0"/>
      <use href="#glassy-wave" x="48" y="3"/>
      <use href="#glassy-wave" x="48" y="5"/>
      <use href="#glassy-wave" x="48" y="7"/>
    </g>
  </svg>
</div>

Three depths

glassy-shallow and glassy-deep move any glass component a step back or forward from the default sheet. They change only the fill, the edge, the blur and the shadow, so they work on cards, panes, banners, fifty-fifties, tables, fieldsets and the author card alike. Use depth to say what to read first; one deep thing per screen is plenty.

<div class="grid-3">
  <article class="card glassy-shallow">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Shallow</h3><p class="card-text">Sits back. A third of the light, a 10px blur, no shadow.</p></div>
  </article>
  <article class="card">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Sheet</h3><p class="card-text">The default every component starts from.</p></div>
  </article>
  <article class="card glassy-deep">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Deep</h3><p class="card-text">Lifts off. Nearly opaque, a 40px blur, a shadow.</p></div>
  </article>
</div>

Pane

A section-sized sheet for grouping smaller glass parts. Inside it, buttons, badges and banners sit on a bigger sheet rather than on the raw gradient. Headings inside a pane are a step smaller than section headings.

<div class="glassy-pane">
  <h2>Opening hours</h2>
  <p>Nine to five, Monday to Friday. Tuesdays and Thursdays are best for visits.</p>
  <div class="row">
    <span class="badge badge-active">Open today</span>
    <button class="btn btn-sm glassy-ghost" type="button">Ring the studio</button>
  </div>
</div>

Float

glassy-float pulls a block up over the one before it by --glassy-overlap (3rem) and draws it on top. The overview uses it to hang three cards off the bottom of the hero; the studio home does the same. Put it on the grid, not on the cards, so the row stays level.

<div class="glassy-pane glassy-deep">
  <p class="kicker">The block above</p>
  <p>Anything with a lower edge for the next block to overlap.</p>
</div>
<div class="grid-2 glassy-float">
  <article class="card">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Pulled up</h3><p class="card-text">By one <code>--glassy-overlap</code>, 3rem, and drawn on top.</p></div>
  </article>
  <article class="card">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">And its neighbour</h3><p class="card-text">The whole grid floats, so the row stays level.</p></div>
  </article>
</div>

Stack

A few panels that belong in an order, each stepped in and laid over the one before, later panels on top. The services page stacks its three services this way, shallowest first. Below 40rem the stack unstacks into an ordinary column.

<div class="glassy-stack">
  <article class="card glassy-shallow">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Websites</h3><p class="card-text">Design and build, four to eight weeks.</p></div>
  </article>
  <article class="card">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Identity</h3><p class="card-text">A mark, a palette, a type system, three weeks.</p></div>
  </article>
  <article class="card glassy-deep">
    <div class="card-body"><h3 class="card-title">Retainers</h3><p class="card-text">A day a week, cancel monthly.</p></div>
  </article>
</div>

Classes

The rules live in css/global.css under /* == flare == */. The last six rows are variants of contract components; their rules are in the same section and their examples on the pages named.

ClassPurpose
.glassy-flareThe wrapper for flare that is only markup (orbs, waves); strip it when swapping templates
.glassy-orbs, .glassy-orbFixed, full-viewport, behind everything; the two children take the two orb tokens
.glassy-waves, .glassy-wave-set useA clipped strip holding the SVG; one wave layer per use
.hero.glassy-sheetThe hero as one deep sheet; pulls a following wave strip up under itself
.glassy-shallow, .glassy-deepDepth tiers for any glass component
.glassy-floatPull a block up over the previous one by --glassy-overlap
.glassy-paneA section-sized sheet for grouping small parts
.glassy-stackPanels stepped in and laid over one another, in order
.glassy-panelThe older free-standing panel; the same recipe as a pane
.card.glassy-shallow, .card.glassy-deepDepth on a card (cards)
.btn.glassy-ghostThe accent as an outline on light glass (buttons)
.banner.glassy-frostA toneless strip of deep glass with the icon in a disc (banner)
.table-wrap.glassy-price, tr.glassy-totalA comparison table with the figures set light and large (table)
.fifty-fifty.glassy-overlapThe picture fills the block and the text panel floats over it (fifty-fifty)
.navbar.glassy-floatingThe bar as a detached sheet inset from the edges (navbar)

Notes

  • The gradient, the three glass fills and edges, the three blur radii, the wave colour, the orb colours and the overlap are all tokens in theme.css (--glassy-*). Nothing in global.css names a colour.
  • The only continuous motion is the orbs' drift and the waves' parallax. Both stop under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce; so does the feature card's tilt, which then only brightens.
  • backdrop-filter is what makes the glass frosted. Every sheet pays for one; on a page with hundreds of cards, lower --glassy-blur or mark them glassy-shallow.
  • The depth, float, pane and stack classes need no wrapper: another template ignores them and shows plain cards in a plain column, which is the intended fallback. Only the orbs and the waves are markup that should be stripped, and they sit in .glassy-flare.
