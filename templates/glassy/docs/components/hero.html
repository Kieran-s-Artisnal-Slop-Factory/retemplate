Component

Hero

The opening of a page: a heading at the largest size, a paragraph, one or two buttons, and beside them whatever the template leads with. Every template has its own hero form; this page shows Glassy's, then two variants that move.

Preview

The hero as the overview uses it. The heading takes --font-size-hero; the paragraph is muted and capped at a readable measure.

<section class="hero glassy-sheet">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">Glass, in three depths</p>
    <h1>Colour behind. Glass in front.</h1>
    <p>Two blurred orbs drift behind the page. Every card, bar and dialog is a sheet of frosted glass over them, and the sheets sit at three depths, so a page is built by layering rather than by stacking. Same markup as every other template here.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg glassy-ghost" href="../../example/index.html">See the studio site</a>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

Variants

Text only

The same hero with nothing beside the words. Add hero-text: the grid becomes one column and the stack is capped at a readable measure. Every template carries this variant with the same class.

<section class="hero hero-text">
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">Words only</p>
    <h1>A hero with nothing beside it.</h1>
    <p>One column, the text measure capped so the lines stay readable, and everything else the hero has: the kicker, the heading at the hero size, the paragraph, the buttons.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg" href="index.html">Browse components</a>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

Image only

A hero that is one picture, edge to edge, cropped to a wide band. Add hero-image and give it a single <img> (or a <figure>) with a real alt, since there is no heading to say what it is. Every template carries this variant with the same class.

<section class="hero hero-image">
  <img src="../../assets/hero.svg" alt="The template's hero picture, filling the width" width="1200" height="500">
</section>

The orbit

Three discs of glass circle the headline on a hairline ring, one turn every forty seconds, staying upright as they go. The text sits in the middle; the discs pass behind it and in front of the orbs. Add glassy-orbit to the hero. Under prefers-reduced-motion the global rule cuts every animation to nothing, so the hero is simply there.

<section class="hero glassy-orbit">
  <div class="glassy-flare glassy-ring" aria-hidden="true"><span></span><span></span><span></span></div>
  <div class="stack">
    <p class="kicker">Three depths, in motion</p>
    <h1>Glass, going round.</h1>
    <p>Three discs on a ring, each a sheet of glass at a different depth, one turn every forty seconds. They never speed up, and under reduced motion they stop where they are.</p>
    <div class="row">
      <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
      <a class="btn btn-lg glassy-ghost" href="../../example/index.html">See the studio site</a>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

The crack

The full-width sheet starts as a plain rectangle. Two cracks draw in from the corners that the default hero cuts, the pane shudders, and the corners break off and fall, leaving the cut shape behind. The falling pieces are pseudo-elements of a wrapper, so the hero's own clip does not take them. Wrap the hero in glassy-crack; the global prefers-reduced-motion rule stops it.

<div class="glassy-flare glassy-crack">
  <svg class="glassy-crack-lines" viewBox="0 0 100 100" preserveAspectRatio="none" aria-hidden="true">
    <path pathLength="1" d="M0 45 L5 42 L8 34 L13 29 L16 18 L20 0"/>
    <path pathLength="1" d="M100 55 L95 59 L92 66 L87 71 L84 82 L80 100"/>
  </svg>
  <section class="hero glassy-sheet">
    <div class="stack">
      <p class="kicker">Under stress</p>
      <h1>Cracked to shape.</h1>
      <p>The sheet begins whole. A crack runs in from each cut corner, the pane shudders, and the two corners fall away and dissolve. What is left is the shape the default hero has. It plays once.</p>
      <div class="row">
        <a class="btn btn-primary btn-lg" href="../index.html">Get started</a>
        <a class="btn btn-lg glassy-ghost" href="../../example/index.html">See the studio site</a>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
</div>

Classes

The rules live in css/global.css: the hero under /* == utilities == */ with the other layout helpers, the variant under /* == hero variants == */.

ClassPurpose
.heroThe opening section: a grid of a text stack and, in most templates, a picture or a piece of flare
.hero .stackThe kicker, heading, paragraph and button row
.hero .rowThe buttons
.hero.glassy-orbitCentred hero with the ring behind it
.glassy-ringThe hairline ring, one turn in forty seconds
.glassy-ring spanA disc; three sizes at three depths, counter-turning to stay upright
.glassy-rings spanA ring; three on two-second offsets
.glassy-crackThe wrapper; its pseudo-elements are the two falling corners
.glassy-crack-linesThe inline SVG whose two paths are the cracks
.glassy-crack > .hero.glassy-sheetThe sheet, cut to shape when the cracks meet the edge
.hero.hero-textWords only: one column, capped measure (every template)
.hero.hero-imagePicture only: one image edge to edge, cropped 21:9 (every template)

Notes

  • One <h1> per page: the hero's heading is it. The kicker above it is a <p>, not a heading.
  • Above 48rem the hero is two columns, text then picture; below, it stacks. The variant keeps whatever layout its markup implies.
  • The variant is CSS only. Its animation is declared with both fill, so the page is correct before it starts and after it ends, and the global prefers-reduced-motion rule stops it.
  • Another template ignores glassy-orbit and shows its own hero; any flare markup inside sits in the template's flare wrapper so a swap can drop it.
